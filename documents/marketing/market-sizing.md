## 3. Addressable Market Estimate

### 3.1 Total Addressable Market (TAM)

All US warehouses and DCs that could theoretically benefit from a pickup queue system:

- **~36,500 facilities** (22,000 warehouses + 14,500 DCs)
- At an average contract value of $300/mo: **$131M ARR**

This represents the maximum theoretical ceiling if every facility adopted the product.

### 3.2 Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM)

Facilities that actively handle customer pickups (will-call counters, loading docks for customer collection). This filters out pure e-commerce fulfillment centers, cold storage, and facilities that only ship outbound:

- **Wholesale distributors** (building materials, electrical, plumbing, industrial, janitorial): ~4,000–6,000 facilities
- **Regional DCs with pickup** (food/beverage, auto parts, HVAC): ~1,000–2,000 facilities
- **Manufacturing with customer pickup** (concrete, lumber, steel): ~500–1,000 facilities

**SAM: 5,500–9,000 facilities**

At an average of $300/mo (blended across Starter and Professional tiers):
- **$19.8M–$32.4M ARR** at full SAM penetration

### 3.3 Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM)

Realistic near-term capture (years 1–3), filtered by:
- NetSuite users (strongest integration fit)
- Tech-forward operations (willing to adopt SaaS)
- Located in top warehouse states (CA, TX, OH, PA, IL, GA, NJ)

**SOM: 500–1,500 facilities** (~5–15% of SAM)

At blended ACV of $300/mo: **$1.8M–$5.4M ARR**

## 5. Industry Tailwinds

### 5.1 BOPIS Normalization → B2B Expectation Transfer
The $129B US BOPIS market (growing to $509B+ by 2033) has trained consumers to expect digital pickup experiences. 97.2 million Americans regularly use BOPIS ([Capital One Shopping](https://capitaloneshopping.com/research/buy-online-pick-up-in-store-statistics/)). B2B buyers — who are the same people in their consumer lives — now expect the same visibility and convenience when picking up from a warehouse.

### 5.2 Warehouse Digital Transformation Is Accelerating
- 61% of companies have achieved their automation goals; two-thirds are actively piloting new initiatives ([McKinsey via Adexin](https://adexin.com/blog/supply-chain-digital-transformation/))
- Cloud adoption in supply chain software projected to reach 82% ([WinSavvy](https://www.winsavvy.com/how-digitized-are-todays-supply-chains-with-adoption-rates/))
- Cloud-based WMS platforms expanding at 19.12% CAGR ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/warehouse-management-system-market))
- Supply chain expenditures increasingly framed as durable infrastructure, not discretionary IT projects ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/supply-chain/2026/earnings-season-made-clear-firms-must-digitize-supply-chains-fall-behind))

### 5.3 Labor Efficiency Pressure
- 17% of warehouse leaders cite demand spike management as a top challenge ([Kardex](https://www.kardex.com/en-us/blog/warehouse-challenges))
- Up to 35% of material-handling tasks may be automated in the near term ([Supply Chain Management Review](https://www.scmr.com/article/2025-warehouse-dc-operations-survey-tech-adoption-marches-on))
- Persistent labor shortages driving investment in operational tools that reduce manual coordination overhead ([SupplySoft](https://www.supplysoft.com/post/warehouse-labor-trends-in-2026))

### 5.4 Inventory & Operations Visibility Gaps
- 30.2% of warehouse leaders identify inventory visibility as their top pain point ([Kardex](https://www.kardex.com/en-us/blog/warehouse-challenges))
- Pickup queue visibility is a closely related gap — customers and staff lack real-time status information
- Real-time data processing demand is a primary WMS growth driver

### 5.5 AI Integration Wave
- Blue Yonder's AI dock scheduling (Jan 2026) cut appointment conflicts by 30% ([Velostics](https://www.velostics.com/blog/agentic-ai-dock-scheduling-yard-management))
- AI-powered wait time predictions and queue optimization becoming table stakes in queue management
- Creates feature expectations that favor modern SaaS entrants over legacy tools

## 6. Industry Headwinds

### 6.1 "Good Enough" Manual Processes
Many warehouses operate with clipboards, radios, and whiteboards. The switching cost is psychological ("we've always done it this way"), not financial. Mitigation: lead with ROI calculators and pilot programs that demonstrate tangible time savings.

### 6.2 ERP Vendor Lock-In Risk
Some prospects may prefer to wait for their ERP vendor (NetSuite, SAP) to add queue features natively rather than adopt a third-party tool. Mitigation: build multi-ERP support and deepen warehouse-specific features that ERP vendors are unlikely to prioritize.

### 6.3 Low Tech Adoption in Traditional Industries
Building materials, industrial supply, and manufacturing tend to be slower technology adopters. Mitigation: target tech-forward distributors first as beachhead customers; offer white-glove onboarding for less tech-savvy operations.

### 6.4 Economic Sensitivity
New software spend may be deferred during economic downturns, especially at the Starter tier where the pain may feel manageable. Mitigation: frame pricing against labor cost savings (the $349/mo tier pays for itself with ~1 hour of saved labor per day) and offer annual discount incentives.

### 6.5 Market Fragmentation
The warehouse operations market is highly fragmented by vertical (building materials, food, auto parts) and by ERP (NetSuite, SAP, Epicor, industry-specific). No single channel reaches the entire SAM. Mitigation: focus deeply on the NetSuite + wholesale distribution segment before expanding.

## 7. Summary

The warehouse pickup queue market is a **niche within a growing ecosystem**. While no single market report sizes this exact category, the convergence of WMS ($4.8B), queue management ($1B+), and dock scheduling ($3–5.6B) — all growing at 10–20%+ CAGRs — validates strong demand for operational tools in the warehouse space.

The realistic near-term opportunity (SOM) of **$1.8M–$5.4M ARR** is achievable by capturing 5–15% of the ~5,500–9,000 US facilities with active customer pickup operations, starting with NetSuite-using wholesale distributors as the beachhead segment.
